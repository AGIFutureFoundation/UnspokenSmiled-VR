The problem

3.58 billion

people affected by oral diseases worldwide — the scale figure used across the project.

Source as labelled in the project: Unspoken Smiles homepage.

~20% of children

have untreated dental decay — the childhood figure behind the school-screening focus.

Source as labelled in the project: CDC, via the project’s Zero Cavity page.

Dental assisting and hygiene skills are normally learned at the chairside, on real patients, with scarce instructor time. Beginners need a place to make their first hundred mistakes where no patient feels them. That place is what this prototype builds: a full clinic in the browser, with the sequence, the checklist, and the reasoning taught before the first real appointment.

Product tour

🦷 The 3D clinic

Chair, light, tray and stations in the browser — walk it with mouse or touch. No install, no account, no headset required.

✅ The 9-step screening session

The flagship prevention and school-screening run: lesson, live checklist beside every run, tasks, and a closing quiz.

🎬 Simulation Studio

Branching clinical decisions scored 2 / 1 / 0, then debriefed — with a patient comfort meter watching force and angle. Simulation ≠ certification, stated in the brief, the debrief, and the portable run record.

🔢 Teeth & Calculus Lab

Study, Quiz, Clean. 32 permanent and 20 primary teeth in FDI notation; choose the right tip for tooth and surface — recognition, not haptics.

🧰 Equipment Lab

12 tools learned by identifying, holding and transferring — plus the 8-stage sterilisation chain in order. Break the order and the load restarts.

🎯 Practice vs. graded

Practice first in the sandbox, then graded runs where sequence is the score. Struggling learners get a hint and a one-tap drop back to the sandbox for that step.

📚 Learning Library

The Fellows’ Resource Hub: encyclopedia, methods, learning paths and checklists — including a plain-language Flow State section.

🌊 Flow State

A visible Flow Meter watches accuracy and pace (Too easy / Flow band / Too hard), adapts difficulty — 85%+ moves up, under 50% moves down, otherwise stay — as advice, never a gate, while Coach Mira narrates.

The pilot offer

The ask is deliberately small:

  • Expert review — a dentist, hygienist or instructor reads the sequences and marks what is wrong.
  • A small pilot cohort — a handful of Fellows, supervised, with feedback collected.
  • A written agreement before any use — no classroom or clinic use on a handshake.

Demo only until then. Try it: live demo · repo · wiki.

What we learned / what we’d do differently

Honesty-first engineering wins

The clearest lesson: our headset button originally said “Enter VR.” It did not enter VR — it reported support. Catching that overclaim and renaming it to “Check headset” before submission mattered more than any feature we shipped that day. Every claim on this page went through the same test: can we point at the thing that proves it?

  • Verify against primary sources. Exam fees, hours, temperatures and guidelines were checked at the source (DANB, CDC, the Dental Board), not copied from summaries — and where sources disagreed, we flagged it instead of picking the nicer number (see Sources, below).
  • Borrow structure, never assets or tone. Cognition.X, SmartCiti.X, STAI Health and Dentist Simulator 2 gave us structures and inspiration only. Nothing of theirs ships in this app.
  • Check the form exists first. Confirm the submission form, its fields and its file limits before the final day — not during it.
  • Video first, not last. Record the demo video at the start of the build, while everything works, instead of leaving CleanShot X to the end. This whitepaper’s storyboard players exist because we learned that the hard way.
  • Connectors before building. Set up accounts, tokens and deploy routes before writing features; mid-build setup costs the hours you no longer have.
  • Push route by file size. Small text changes go by the fast snapshot route; large builds go by upload. Mixing them up wasted a cycle we could not spare.

Sources & honesty notes

  • Conflicts flagged, not hidden: project sources disagree on a founding year (2014 vs 2011) and on a people-reached figure (10,000 vs 9,000+). Both versions are noted here rather than silently resolved; the primary source should settle each before any public claim is made.
  • Inspiration only: STAI Health and Dentist Simulator 2 informed structure and ideas. No assets, text, or branding from them are used, and neither endorses this prototype.
  • Licensure figures (DANB CDA $450 / 195 minutes / 245 questions; California RDA 800 hours = 275 + 260 + 265; CDC MMWR 2003;52(RR17)) are stated as published by those bodies; check the current official pages before relying on them, as fees and rules change.
  • Video status: both explainers on this page are storyboard players with browser narration, not recordings. The CleanShot X recording is still to be done.
  • Headset status: “Check headset” reports support and starts no session. Immersive WebXR is Phase 4, not built, with no Quest test performed.
